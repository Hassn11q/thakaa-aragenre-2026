\begin{abstract}
We describe the winning system for the AraGenre~2026 shared task on hierarchical,
definition-guided Arabic genre classification, where each of 27{,}972 hidden-test
Arabic segments must be assigned one of 6 broad genres and one of 74 fine-grained
specific genres whose English definitions are provided but whose specific-label inventory is
\emph{disjoint} from the released training data, the 6 broad genres being shared. Our approach uses
no task training. A \textbf{family-restricted, definition-guided LLM judge} performs
setwise scoring of candidate genres within a hierarchically constrained decoding
scheme, reaching 0.7139 Hierarchical Macro~F1. From the public scoreboard we diagnose
that our system already led on Specific Macro~F1 but trailed on Broad Macro~F1, and we
narrow that gap with a \textbf{full-context multi-model consensus} correction stage:
two instruction models are each shown the \emph{entire} 74-definition taxonomy
and re-predict the specific genre; corrections are applied only where both models agree
against the base system. Feeding the full taxonomy makes the models far more resistant to a topic trap (confusing
a book \emph{about} religion with religious scripture). A final attractor-drain step redistributes over-predicted generic classes to starved rare
classes. The system scores \textbf{0.7352 Hierarchical Macro~F1, ranking 1st}, $+0.018$
over the next team and improving every metric over our base.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
AraGenre~2026~\citep{aragenre2026} frames Arabic genre classification as a
generalisation problem:
systems receive English genre definitions and limited synthetic training data, but the
hidden benchmark contains noisier naturally occurring Arabic spanning Modern Standard
Arabic, Classical Arabic, and multiple dialects, with \emph{previously unseen
genre--definition combinations}. Because the 74 specific test genres are disjoint from
training, memorisation is useless and semantic understanding of communicative function
is required. The official ranking metric is
\[
\text{Hier.\ F1} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\text{Broad F1}_{\text{macro}} + \text{Spec.\ F1}_{\text{macro}}\right).
\]

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0em
\item A hierarchically constrained, definition-guided LLM judge that reaches 0.7139 Hierarchical Macro~F1 with no task training.
\item A scoreboard-driven diagnosis showing the two tiers must be optimised differently,
and that our deficit against the leading system sat entirely in the broad tier.
\item A full-context multi-model consensus correction, applied conservatively (agreement-gated,
direction-verified), with a three-arm ablation separating the effect of the taxonomy from
that of an explicit type-vs-topic instruction.
\item An attractor-drain step targeting the macro-F1 failure mode of over-predicting
generic classes.
\item A winning result: 0.7352 Hierarchical Macro~F1, rank~1 of 18 teams, with each component
scored as a submission.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Code and prompts: \url{https://github.com/Hassan11q/thakaa-aragenre-2026}. The main
challenges were a fully disjoint label set, a topic trap (writing \emph{about} a subject
vs.\ the subject's genre), and classes with no written signal (song dialects, textbook levels).

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Arabic and hierarchy-aware HTC.} Arabic pretrained models~\citep{antoun2020arabert,antoun2021aragpt2}
underpin Arabic classification, including hierarchical variants~\citep{aragpt2htc2025,arabichtc2026}, and a large
literature models the label hierarchy explicitly~\citep{zhu2023hitin,cai2023hpt,zeng2025cpm,wang2025hierclassifier,combining2025,htcsurvey2024}.
These closed-label supervised setups do not transfer to AraGenre's unseen, definition-specified labels; we instead
enforce the hierarchy structurally, forcing $\text{broad}=\text{parent}(\text{specific})$.

\paragraph{Zero-shot HTC, LLM judging, and consensus.} Zero-shot HTC matches text to label semantics rather than
fixed classes; prompt-ensembling~\citep{xia2025hieps}, LLM-enhanced prototypes~\citep{zhang2024hierprompt},
LLM-refined taxonomies~\citep{golde2025refined}, consistency regularisation~\citep{consistencyreg2024} and
reasoning-based HTC~\citep{jiang2025reasoning} inform our definition refinement and chain-of-thought pass. Our
two-stage design retrieves candidate definitions~\citep{chen2024retrieval} then compares them with an
LLM~\citep{sun2023rankgpt,zheng2023judging}, using Qwen3-Embedding~\citep{qwen3embedding2025} to bridge Arabic text
and English definitions. Combining independent verdicts builds on ensembling~\citep{dietterich2000ensemble} and its
LLM forms: juries~\citep{verga2024poll}, blending~\citep{jiang2023llmblender}, mixtures of
agents~\citep{wang2024moa}, debate~\citep{du2023debate}, and self-consistency~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}.
Unlike juries voting on free-form text, we require \emph{exact-label} agreement, and find it
reliable only with the full taxonomy in context.

\section{Background}
Each instance is \texttt{\{id, text\}}; systems output one \texttt{broad\_genre} (of 6:
Informative, Creative, Interactive, Learning, Legal, Religious) and one
\texttt{specific\_genre} (of 74), using exact taxonomy label names, each with an English
definition. The released data are tiny and synthetic-like (train: 140 items / 7 specific genres;
dev: 110 / 6), and the specific-label inventories are \emph{100\% disjoint} across train,
dev and test, so no supervised label set transfers. The \emph{hidden test} has 27{,}972 naturally occurring instances over 74 specific
genres in 6 families: Informative (42, dominated by 20 job domains and 13 book-description
topics), Learning (12: textbooks by level, student writing by topic), Creative (7: 5 song
dialects, classical vs.\ MSA poetry), Religious (6: Islamic and Christian), Interactive (4)
and Legal (3). Missing predictions are counted
incorrect; official dev baselines are in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. Informative dominates ($\sim$50\% of predictions), and the specific
tier's 74 classes make Macro~F1 highly sensitive to rare-class recall (e.g., dialect song
lyrics, niche job domains).

\subsection{Base: Family-Restricted Definition-Guided Judge}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} summarises the pipeline. A locally served open Gemma-family model~\citep{gemma2024}, exposed by our vLLM endpoint
under the identifier \texttt{gemma-4-31b}, acts as a setwise judge; we report the served
identifier verbatim and pin it, together with the API model strings, in
Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}. For each text:
(i) \texttt{Qwen3-Embedding-8B}~\citep{qwen3embedding2025} encodes the Arabic text and the
English candidate definitions and proposes candidate specific genres by cosine similarity,
bridging Arabic text and English definitions; (ii) decoding is hierarchically
constrained: the broad genre is gated first and specific candidates are restricted to
that broad family (all siblings for small families, top-$k$ retrieved for large topical
ones), so that $\text{broad}=\text{parent}(\text{specific})$ and broad \emph{accuracy} equals the
family-accuracy of the specific prediction (Broad Macro~F1 does not: it averages per-class
F1, and the gap between the two is what \S\ref{sec:diag} exploits); (iii) the judge scores each candidate 0--100
against its definition in one setwise call~\citep{sun2023rankgpt} and we take the argmax after
a light marginal correction (Appendix~\ref{app:calib}); and (iv) a few
high-precision surface rules override the judge only on near-unambiguous markers
(Qur'anic mushaf orthography $\rightarrow$ \texttt{quran}; isn\=ad
openings $\rightarrow$ \texttt{hadith}; both estimated to fire at $>$98\% precision by
inspecting a sample of firings, as the test split has no gold; emoji/hashtag short posts $\rightarrow$
Interactive subtypes). We also supply 74 offline-generated per-genre contrastive
discriminators; hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}. On the hidden test this base pipeline scores \textbf{0.7139}.

\subsection{Diagnosis from the Scoreboard}
\label{sec:diag}
The leaderboard exposed the structure (Table~\ref{tab:metrics}): our base led on
Specific Macro~F1 (0.5487 vs.\ 0.5339) but trailed on Broad Macro~F1 (0.8792 vs.\ 0.90).
As the ranking metric is their mean, the entire deficit was in the broad tier. Broad
accuracy (0.9003) exceeding broad macro (0.8792) indicated a minority broad class dragged
the mean via cross-family false positives: Bible passages filed as poetry or encyclopedia
entries, news filed
as legal, informal comments filed as book descriptions.

\subsection{Full-Context Multi-Model Consensus Correction}
We re-predict the specific genre with two frontier instruction models
(GPT-5.6~Luna~\citep{gpt56luna2026} and Gemini-3.6~Flash~\citep{gemini36flash2026}),
\emph{feeding each the entire 74-definition taxonomy} ($\approx$2.9K tokens) grouped by
broad genre, and asking for the single best specific label. This follows the classical ensemble argument that combining diverse,
independently-erring predictors beats any single predictor~\citep{dietterich2000ensemble}, in its LLM
form~\citep{verga2024poll,wang2024moa}. (A third model, Claude~Sonnet~5~\citep{sonnet5_2026}, was
piloted but excluded on cost grounds.)

\paragraph{What suppresses the topic trap.} With only the 6 broad definitions,
all models fell into a \emph{topic trap}: text \emph{about} Islam $\rightarrow$ Religious,
even when it was an Informative \emph{book description}. The full-taxonomy prompt also carries an explicit type-vs-topic instruction, so we ran a
three-arm ablation over the same 1{,}403 book-description instances to separate the two factors
(one model, GPT-5.6, identical pool): with broad definitions only and no such instruction, 394 are
called Religious; adding the instruction alone removes 41\% of those (394$\rightarrow$233); supplying
the full taxonomy removes a further 49\% (233$\rightarrow$119). Both factors matter and neither
explains the effect alone: the instruction states the convention, the taxonomy shows the label that
realises it. Scripture filed as an encyclopedia entry (\ar{هؤلاء امراء بني عيسو}, Genesis~36) is moved to
\texttt{bible}, and base-family agreement rises in five of the six families (Appendix~\ref{app:trap}).

\paragraph{Conservative application.} The first model runs over all 27{,}972 instances, the
second only over the 8{,}041 where the first disagrees with the base, and a correction is
applied only where both name the same label. Broad-changing moves are gated to seven
read-verified directions, led by Informative$\rightarrow$Interactive for comments and
Legal$\rightarrow$Informative for wire news (Appendix~\ref{app:subs}); unreliable directions
(e.g.\ Informative$\rightarrow$Learning, which over-fires student-writing on academic prose)
are rejected, 718 moves in all. Within-family refinements are applied wherever both models
agree.

\subsection{Attractor Drain (Specific Macro)}
Manual reading revealed five large ``attractor'' classes that absorb texts the judge cannot
place confidently, inflating them and starving rare siblings. The drain is deterministic: when the base label is an attractor class and an independent
chain-of-thought re-judge~\citep{wei2022cot} names a different sibling in the same family, the
sibling replaces it (broad never changes). The surface rules are then re-applied over the
corrected labels, acting as a guard rather than a gain (Appendix~\ref{app:subs}). On the base alone it moves 554 instances, 6--35\%
of each attractor class (Appendix~\ref{app:drain}); in the final pipeline consensus runs
first, leaving the drain 383. Relative to the base, the final system changes 459 broad and
2{,}890 specific labels (10.3\% of instances).


\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Data splits.} We train nothing; every system is applied unchanged to the hidden test. An input is \texttt{\{"id","text"\}} with Arabic text such as
\ar{مطلوب سائق باص 24 راكب في شركة في دبي جبل علي} (test id 24486, ``bus driver
wanted, 24-seater, for a company in Dubai, Jebel Ali''); the required output pairs it with
\texttt{broad\_genre} = Informative and \texttt{specific\_genre} =
\texttt{drivers\_and\_} \texttt{delivery\_jobs}.
\paragraph{Preprocessing.} None. Normalisation erases the two orthographic signals that
separate whole families here: heavy diacritisation marks 77\% of the texts we label
\texttt{quran} and 43\% of poetry against 1\% elsewhere, and emoji mark 47\% of Interactive
posts against under 1\% (Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}).
\paragraph{Tools.} \texttt{transformers}, \texttt{sentence-transformers} and
\texttt{bitsandbytes} for Qwen3-Embedding-8B, vLLM for the local judge, and the \texttt{openai}
and \texttt{google-genai} SDKs for the verifiers; versions and URLs in
Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}.
\paragraph{Metrics.} Macro F1, Weighted F1 and Accuracy at both tiers; the official ranking metric is
Hierarchical Macro F1, the mean of Broad and Specific Macro F1.
\paragraph{Cost.} Consensus sends a $\approx$3.0K-token prompt per instance, about
US\$1.05--1.48 per 1{,}000 instances and \$50 in total; wall-clock timings are in
Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}. The base pipeline needs no paid API.

\subsection{Development-Set Results and Component Transfer}
\label{sec:dev}
Re-running the pipeline on dev (110 examples, official gold) separates what transfers from
what does not. The drain transfers: it reads its attractor classes off the prediction
distribution alone, with no class list carried over -- the two it fires on run at $1.30\times$
and $1.25\times$ gold frequency -- and moves five labels for $+0.042$. The consensus stage does not: dev has no
\texttt{*\_book\_description} and no scripture, so the trap it repairs has zero instances; two
corrections fire and both are wrong. Residual dev errors stem from a convention clash between
splits (Appendix~\ref{app:convention}).



\section{Analysis}
Broad Macro is dominated by minority-class false positives, Specific Macro by rare-class
recall, so gains on both compound through the mean. Broad-only prompting
produced 157 topic-trap errors on the book descriptions that all three models we tried made
unanimously, which the full
taxonomy dissolved; and any single strong model disagreed with the base on $\sim$29\% of
specifics, too noisy to trust, whereas two-model exact-label agreement filtered this to
reliable corrections. A direct six-way broad classifier regressed to 0.6206 and unpruned
74-way scoring to 0.6517; self-consistency, optimal transport and uniform-prior calibration
all fell below the 0.7139 base (Appendix~\ref{app:negative}).

\paragraph{Error analysis (hidden test).} Three types dominate: cross-family topic leaks (a description
\emph{of} a religious book attracting Religious, and scripture misfiled as fiction), attractor
over-prediction, and ceiling classes where 87--99\% of the texts we label as song lyrics carry no marker from
that dialect's own cue list. Examples in
Appendix~\ref{app:errors}.

\section{Conclusion}
Zero-shot hierarchical Arabic genre classification is achievable without task training: a
constrained definition-guided judge, an agreement-gated consensus and a rare-class attractor
drain ranked 1st at 0.7352 Hierarchical Macro~F1. Our ablation shows the topic trap is
suppressed jointly by a type-vs-topic instruction and by the full label inventory, neither
alone. The remaining ceilings are song-lyric dialect and textbook reading level.

\section*{Limitations}
Every hidden-test \emph{score} we report is an official evaluation-phase submission
(Appendix~\ref{app:subs}); we made no submission after the deadline. Two kinds of number are
not submissions and should not be read as such. The instruction-only arm of the three-arm
ablation (\S\ref{sec:system}) was run after the evaluation phase closed, so that comparison is
post hoc; and the descriptive tables in Appendices~\ref{app:errors}, \ref{app:trap},
\ref{app:drain} and~\ref{app:subs} are analyses of the submitted outputs, computed locally
rather than scored.
The hidden test has no released gold labels and the development set is near-degenerate, so
component decisions were validated on the public leaderboard and by manual reading rather
than on held-out gold. Our reported progression therefore doubles as the selection signal:
successive submissions were guided by leaderboard feedback, which risks fitting to the test
distribution, and we report no held-out confidence intervals or significance tests. The
gains should be read as leaderboard-measured rather than statistically certified, and, absent gold, we can offer no uncertainty estimate on the winning margin. Consensus correction incurs API cost
proportional to test size. Song-dialect distinctions remain near the noise floor because
the distinguishing features are phonetic and largely absent from written lyrics.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
\textbf{Official submission.} The results we report are those of submission 872515,
evaluated 1~August 2026, which scored 0.7352 Hierarchical Macro~F1 (Specific Macro 0.5725,
Weighted 0.6125, Accuracy 0.6189; Broad Macro 0.8979, Weighted 0.9142, Accuracy 0.9138) over
27{,}972 of 27{,}972 gold instances with no missing prediction, ranking 1st of the 18 teams in
the final standings (\texttt{artifacts/final\_leaderboard.tsv}). Every hidden-test number in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Appendix~\ref{app:negative} is likewise an official scored
submission rather than a local estimate; the submission IDs are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:subs}.
All inference is zero-shot with no task-specific training. \textbf{Data:} we use only the
official AraGenre inputs and definition files, and no external labelled data. The consensus
verifiers receive the 74 definitions verbatim. The judge does not: by default it appends a
one-line Arabic discriminator to each definition and prepends per-family cue lists, both
author-written from the released definitions and Arabic linguistic knowledge, and both naming
genres explicitly (\texttt{src/discriminators.py}, \texttt{src/judge.py}). The surface rules
name a further seven labels. No test label or annotation informs any of it, but the pipeline is
not label-agnostic and we do not claim it is. \textbf{Models:} the base retriever is Qwen3-Embedding-8B (4-bit NF4
weights with fp16 compute, 4096-dim, cosine over L2-normalised vectors, sequence length
capped at 512 tokens); the judge is a Gemma-family model
(\texttt{gemma-4-31b}) served via vLLM with setwise 0--100 scoring; the consensus verifiers
are GPT-5.6~Luna and Gemini-3.6~Flash, each queried with the full-taxonomy prompt.
\textbf{Settings:} the judge decodes at temperature 0 with seed 42; the Gemini verifier runs at
temperature 0 and the GPT verifier at \texttt{reasoning\_effort=low} with no temperature set.
The retriever caches the top 20 specific genres per text; the judge scores every sibling in the
four small families and the top 6 retrieved elsewhere, in a single setwise pass, with marginal
correction $\alpha{=}0.5$. \textbf{Pipeline and code:} the retriever, judge,
full-context consensus verifiers (\texttt{src/verify.py}), chain-of-thought drain, and the
submission builder (\texttt{src/submission.py}, which enforces
$\text{broad}{=}\text{parent}(\text{specific})$ and validates that all 27{,}972 IDs are
present with exact labels) are released in the project repository. \textbf{Two cached inputs.} The broad gate the judge restricts to is submission 866538
(0.6812), an earlier retrieval-gated system we submitted and scored, reused verbatim rather
than regenerated by the released code; and the base
predictions additionally carry an Interactive-recall reassignment applied during the
evaluation phase by a separate automatic judge pass (\texttt{src/interactive\_judge.py}), which
we release but which is not wired into \texttt{reproduce.sh}; re-running the released judge over the
same candidate sets reproduces 93.5\% of the base specific labels and 97.1\% of the broad
labels. Both files ship in \texttt{artifacts/}, so the submitted predictions rebuild exactly
(27{,}972/27{,}972) from cached outputs, but the base stage is a cached input rather than
something the repository regenerates end to end. \textbf{Caveats:} the
hosted API models are versioned by release date and are not guaranteed bit-reproducible
across dates; the consensus stage incurs API cost proportional to the number of verified
instances (on the order of tens of US dollars for this test set). The base pipeline runs on
a single CUDA GPU (embeddings) plus a remote vLLM endpoint (judge).

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work uses only the publicly released AraGenre shared-task data and introduces no new
data collection; we did not gather, store, or infer personal information, and any
user-generated text (e.g., social-media posts) originates from the official benchmark. In
line with the shared-task rules, all predictions are produced automatically by the system:
no submitted label was hand-edited and we did not reverse-engineer the hidden evaluation
labels. We did read model outputs and the corresponding texts to design automatic
correction rules, and the rule-precision estimates in \S\ref{sec:system} come from that
inspection; this is author inspection of system outputs, not annotation of the evaluation
set, and no such judgement enters a submission except through a deterministic rule.

Genre classification is a comparatively low-risk task, but two considerations warrant care.
First, the taxonomy spans religiously and culturally sensitive categories (Islamic and
Christian scripture, dialectal varieties, regional song lyrics), and we treat these
descriptively as text-type/register labels; misclassification carries no endorsement or
value judgement, and the system is not intended to assess the authenticity, orthodoxy, or
quality of religious or cultural texts. Second, dialect-related labels can encode regional
and social signals; models trained on skewed corpora may under-serve lower-resourced
dialects (e.g., the near-noise-floor separation of Sudanese and Iraqi song lyrics we
report), which practitioners should weigh before deployment.

Our correction stage sends Arabic text to third-party hosted LLM APIs; because the inputs
are already-public shared-task data, this poses no additional privacy exposure, but the
same pipeline should not be applied to private or sensitive text without review. The
approach relies on large hosted models whose availability, cost, and behaviour may change,
which limits equitable reproducibility; the base retriever-plus-open-judge pipeline is
provided as a lower-cost, self-hostable alternative.

\section{Annotation-Convention Divergence Between Splits}
\label{app:convention}
Five of the seven residual dev errors are texts that analyse religion from the outside, e.g.
\ar{تشير بعض الدراسات إلى أن أساليب الوعظ تختلف بين المجتمعات} (``some studies indicate that
preaching styles differ between societies''). Our system labels these \texttt{Analytical
Reports} (Informative); the dev gold label is \texttt{Religious Commentary} (Religious).

This is the \emph{inverse} of the convention in the hidden test, where
\texttt{islamic\_book\_description} (``texts summarising Islamic or religious books'') is
Informative and our consensus stage is rewarded for moving such texts out of Religious. Both
conventions are stated in their own definition files, and the two files disagree about where
writing \emph{about} religion belongs. A system that follows the test convention is therefore
necessarily penalised on dev, which bounds how far dev accuracy can be pushed without
split-specific rules. It also supports our central claim: the convention is carried by the
definitions, which is why supplying the full taxonomy, rather than the broad labels alone,
changes model behaviour so sharply.
\section{Error Analysis Examples}
\label{app:errors}
Three error types dominate. (i)~\emph{Cross-family topic leaks}: a description \emph{of} a
religious book (\ar{يهدف هذا الكتاب إلى قراءة سيرة الرسول}, id 10041) is
\texttt{islamic\_book\_description} (Informative), which our base and final systems both get
right but which every broad-only verifier calls Religious; scripture leaks the other way
(\ar{هؤلاء امراء بني عيسو}, Genesis~36, id 10798), where the base says
\texttt{history\_encyclopedia} and consensus corrects it to \texttt{bible}. (ii)~\emph{Attractor over-prediction}: long prose absorbed into \texttt{egyptian\_song\_lyrics}.
(iii)~\emph{Ceiling classes}: for each of the five song-lyric classes, 87--99\% of the texts we
assign to it contain no marker from that dialect's cue list
(\texttt{src/dialect\_markers.py} in our repository ships the lists and reproduces the table;
the rate is a property of the text, so it is measurable without gold).


\section{Negative Results}
\label{app:negative}
A \emph{direct} six-way broad classifier regressed to 0.6206, confirming
that deriving broad from the specific prediction beats classifying it directly; scoring all 74 candidates without
retrieval pruning fell to 0.6517. For correction, plain self-consistency (0.7134), entropic optimal transport
(0.7115), uniform-prior calibration (0.7124) and an unrestricted chain-of-thought pass (0.7131)
all regressed below the 0.7139 base, whereas full-context consensus improved it. Cross-lingual rerankers failed~\citep{btzsc2026}, regex rules added nothing the judge lacked, and
the off-the-shelf dialect classifiers we tried did not expose the Iraqi/Sudanese
distinction that NADI-style country-level tasks define~\citep{nadi2021}.

\section{Calibration}
\label{app:calib}
The correction subtracts a scaled per-genre mean from each candidate's score,
$\tilde{s}(y) = s(y) - \alpha\,\bar{s}(y)$, where $\bar{s}(y)$ is the mean score genre $y$
received over the whole test set and $\alpha{=}0.5$. It damps genres the judge rates generously
everywhere, which is what starves rare siblings. We started from $\alpha{=}0.75$, chosen on the
released training labels; it over-flattened once the label space grew from 7 to 74 classes.
The correction is applied before the argmax and never changes the broad family. Two
alternatives we tried instead of it, uniform-prior calibration and entropic optimal transport,
both scored below the base (Appendix~\ref{app:negative}).

\section{Hyperparameters and Prompts}
\label{app:hparams}
Retriever: Qwen3-Embedding-8B loaded in 4-bit NF4 with fp16 compute (the configuration the
submitted run used; only the cosine ranking is consumed and NF4 preserves embedding
direction), 4096-dim, L2-normalised, \texttt{max\_seq\_length} 512, the top 20 specific genres
cached per text; the judge scores every sibling in the four small families (Creative 7,
Religious 6, Interactive 4, Legal 3) and the top 6 retrieved in-family candidates in the two
large ones. Judge: \texttt{gemma-4-31b} via vLLM, setwise 0--100 scoring in a single pass,
temperature 0, seed 42, 3 retries with JSON-regex fallback, marginal correction $\alpha{=}0.5$.
Consensus verifiers: GPT-5.6~Luna (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=low}, $\leq$500 completion tokens) and
Gemini-3.6~Flash (temperature 0, $\leq$400 output tokens), each given the full 74-definition taxonomy
(2{,}884 tokens under \texttt{o200k\_base}, giving a mean prompt of 3{,}034 tokens and a maximum
of 5{,}185) in the system prompt, and the text truncated to 1500 characters. The judge prompt asks for a
JSON array of integer scores over the candidate definitions; the consensus prompt asks for a single exact
\texttt{specific\_genre} identifier and states that a factual description \emph{of} a book is a
\texttt{*\_book\_description}, not the book's subject genre.

\paragraph{Throughput.} A consensus call takes 2.0--2.8\,s. At the released default of 20
concurrent workers the 8{,}041-instance Gemini pass took 19\,min of wall clock; the full GPT
pass over 27{,}972 instances was run at higher concurrency.

\paragraph{Two readings this record supports.} Submission 867465 isolates the surface rules on
top of 867323: they rewrite 32 labels (23 \texttt{quran}, 9 \texttt{hadith}) and leave the
score at 0.6881 against 0.6882. The rules are precise but score-neutral at that point, so the
0.6882$\rightarrow$0.7139 gain belongs to the judge and calibration changes, not to them.
They were kept for a different job: in the final pipeline they fire on 1{,}087 instances and
agree with the base label on every one, so their whole net effect is to restore 75 labels that
consensus or the drain had moved away (56 \texttt{youtube\_comments}, 19 \texttt{quran}). They
guard the correction stages rather than adding score.
Submission 871127 applies the chain-of-thought pass to every instance and scores 0.7131,
below the 0.7139 base; the same pass restricted to attractor classes is what the drain uses.
Chain-of-thought helps here only where it is aimed.

\paragraph{Gated broad moves.} Of the 3{,}300 instances where both verifiers agree against the
base, 2{,}122 stay inside the base family and are applied as refinements. Of the 1{,}178 that
would cross families, 460 fall in a read-verified direction and are applied:
Informative$\rightarrow$Interactive 195, Legal$\rightarrow$Informative 80,
Creative$\rightarrow$Religious 58, Informative$\rightarrow$Religious 58,
Creative$\rightarrow$Informative 44, Interactive$\rightarrow$Religious 20,
Legal$\rightarrow$Interactive 5. The remaining 718 are rejected, the largest groups being
Informative$\rightarrow$Learning (219), Creative$\rightarrow$Interactive (122) and
Learning$\rightarrow$Informative (99).

\paragraph{Preserved rule defects.} Three regexes in \texttt{src/rules.py} do not match their
intent: the Qur'anic class also admits U+0671, the opinion class lets a bare variation selector
match, and one character class contains a literal \texttt{|}. They ran as written when the
submission was produced, so we keep them verbatim and document them in the code; correcting all
three changes 22 of the 27{,}972 final predictions.

($n{=}647$), book descriptions 1\%/0\% ($n{=}3{,}916$), Interactive 0\%/47\% ($n{=}1{,}103$).
Both are properties of the text, so no gold is needed;
\texttt{src/orthographic\_signals.py} reproduces the table.
